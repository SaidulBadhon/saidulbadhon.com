A customer asks for your SOC 2 report, you don't have one, and you need to know where to start. This guide walks through the whole thing in [Jutsu](https://jutsu.ai), one screen at a time: creating your workspace, answering setup, publishing policies, doing the recurring evidence work, connecting your systems, tracking your score and getting ready for the auditor.

Full disclosure: I'm the lead engineer at Jutsu and helped build this module. Every screenshot below is from a real workspace, Jutsu Inc., that I set up from scratch for this guide. The one exception is marked as example data. If you'd rather watch than read, the same journey is in an [11 minute walkthrough video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=306cqZcS9D4).

## Before you start

You need three things:

- **A Jutsu workspace on a paid plan.** Compliance comes with every paid plan, and every paid plan includes all 13 frameworks. The Free plan doesn't include it.
- **Admin access.** Only an admin can run compliance setup.
- **Time for 13 short questions.** Answers save as you go, so you can stop and come back later on any device.

It helps to have a few facts ready: who runs the company and their titles, who will sign for the company at the audit, how your team signs in to work apps, where your product runs, and the main software your team uses.

## Step 3: Answer the 13 setup questions

Setup asks 13 questions, one per screen. Every answer changes what Jutsu builds, so here is what each one does. (Your company name and website come from the workspace, which is why there are 13 and not 15.)

### Question 1: Which frameworks do you need?

Pick the frameworks your customers ask for. For most startups that's SOC 2, often with ISO 27001. Jutsu sets each framework up the moment you continue: its requirements, its controls, its policy drafts and its recurring tasks. Nothing is published yet.

The panel on the right keeps track of the program you're building. For SOC 2 plus ISO 27001, setup prepared 32 policy drafts, 86 recurring tasks and 25 company records.

<figure>
  <Image src={shot04Q1Frameworks} alt="Setup question 1 of 13, 'Which frameworks do you need?', with six framework cards (SOC 2 and ISO 27001 selected, ISO 42001, HIPAA, GDPR and PCI DSS unselected), a 'Set up 2 frameworks' button, and a right-hand panel listing 'Your program: SOC 2, ISO 27001' above 'After the last question'." sizes="(min-width: 768px) 768px, 100vw" placeholder="blur" />
  <figcaption>Question 1: pick SOC 2 and ISO 27001. The right-hand panel keeps a running list of your program.</figcaption>
</figure>

Tip: if you need two frameworks, pick both now. Controls are shared between frameworks, so work you do once counts for both.

### Questions 2 to 4: Your company

Describe what your company does in two or three sentences, the way you'd explain it to a new customer. Jutsu tries to draft this from your website, and if it can't, you write it yourself. Jutsu AI uses this to tailor your policy drafts, so be specific.

Then pick your industry and enter roughly how many people work at the company. A rough number is fine.

## Step 5: Let Jutsu build your program

Jutsu now builds the program in six steps:

1. Drafts your policies, filling in your company facts and rewording the instructions to match how you work, without weakening any rule.
2. Adds the vendors it found in your answers.
3. Suggests risks.
4. Links those risks and vendors to your recurring work.
5. Drafts treatment plans where there's something to plan.
6. Writes the company description your auditor reads first.

For our example company this took under four minutes, and you can leave the page while it runs. When it's done, **Setup** shows what it built. If your company changes a lot later, come back and click **Run setup again**.

Nothing is published, assigned or accepted yet. Everything waits as a draft or a suggestion until a person acts on it.

## Step 6: Understand frameworks, requirements and controls

Before you start the work, it helps to know how the pieces fit together:

- A **framework**, like SOC 2, is a list of **requirements**.
- A requirement is met by **controls**.
- A control is met by **work**: a policy, a recurring evidence task, or a company record.

Open a framework from the sidebar to see how far along you are and what's left.

<figure>
  <Image src={shot13Soc2Page} alt="The SOC 2 framework page with a 0% ring and the headline 'You are 0% of the way to SOC 2.', '61 requirements left', '0 assigned to you', a 'Next up for SOC 2' card, and an 'Audit timeline' section with 'SOC 2 Type 1 audit' (about 25 weeks, 8 phases) and 'SOC 2 Type 2 audit' (about 38 weeks, 9 phases), each with a Start button." sizes="(min-width: 768px) 768px, 100vw" placeholder="blur" />
  <figcaption>The SOC 2 page: requirements left, and the two audit timelines you can start.</figcaption>
</figure>

Open a requirement to see a plain-language summary of what it asks for, and the controls that meet it. Here, SOC 2 CC6.1 (logical access security) is met by 10 controls.

<figure>
  <Image src={shot14RequirementCc61} alt="A requirement page titled 'Logical access security' (Not started), 'CC6.1 Logical and physical access controls', a plain-language summary, and a 'Controls that meet it, 0 of 10 done' list starting with JC-001 Asset inventory maintained, JC-011 MFA enforced for all users and JC-012 MFA enforced for privileged access." sizes="(min-width: 768px) 768px, 100vw" placeholder="blur" />
  <figcaption>Open a requirement (here CC6.1) to see the controls that meet it.</figcaption>
</figure>

Open a control to see the work behind it and every requirement it meets, grouped by framework. "MFA enforced for all users" counts for SOC 2 CC6.1 and ISO 27001 A.8.5 at the same time, so finishing its work moves both frameworks.

<figure>
  <Image src={shot15ControlRequirements} alt="A card titled 'Requirements it meets' with two groups: SOC 2, CC6.1 Logical access security (Not started), and ISO 27001, A.8.5 Secure authentication (Not started)." sizes="(min-width: 768px) 768px, 100vw" placeholder="blur" />
  <figcaption>One control counts toward requirements in both frameworks, so you do the work once.</figcaption>
</figure>

The rules are simple. A control is done when all of its work is done. A requirement is met when every control under it is done. A control with no work linked to it is flagged as a gap.

## Step 8: Do the recurring evidence work

Go to **All work → Evidence**. Evidence is a set of recurring tasks: some monthly, some quarterly, some yearly. Each period gets its own record.

Open a task to see what this period needs, how to do it, who owns it, and which controls it proves. Finish it by clicking **Add evidence** (upload a file or write a note), then **Mark as done**.

  <Image src={shot21EvidenceTask} alt="The evidence task 'Confirm multi-factor sign-in is enforced' with a To do badge, 'Add evidence' and 'Mark as done' buttons, a 'This period' card (Oct 17 to Nov 16, 2026, 31 days left, 'Nothing added yet'), and a 'How it is run' card (Who does it Alex Morgan, Approval is off, Repeats Every month, Team IT, Next period Nov 17, 2026)." sizes="(min-width: 768px) 768px, 100vw" placeholder="blur" />

The period is now done, and the next one starts on its date. Closed periods never change. If a period ends before the work is done, it's marked missed and stays on the record.

Send it for review. Once someone other than the author approves it, it counts for a set time (six months for an access review).

## Step 12: Connect your systems so Jutsu checks them every day

For our example company, Amazon Web Services alone could keep 27 tasks current. Start with the system most of your work runs on. Each connection takes a few minutes, and Jutsu only reads.

The **Overview** shows one number: how far along you are to the frameworks you picked. Click **How this is measured** to see exactly how it's counted. It's the average of four parts: policies, evidence, documents and people.

The score counts work your team has finished, not audit results, and anything marked not relevant is left out. Our example company went from 0% to 2% on its first day after one published policy, one finished evidence task and one approved access review. ## Quick checklist

- Pick a paid plan and run setup (13 questions).
- Pick every framework you need at the start, so shared work is done once.
- Fill the blanks in your policies, then send them for approval and publish them.
- Invite your team so they can sign policies and finish training.
- Add the suggested risks and vendors that apply, and accept their checks.
- Connect your cloud and code so daily checks collect evidence for you.
- Close each evidence period on time.
- Start your audit timeline, invite your auditor, and publish your trust center.

## Where to go from here

The first day gets you a program: drafts, tasks and records, all waiting for a person. Getting audit-ready is the work that follows, period after period, and the score only moves when that work is real. If you'd like to see it before you try it, the [walkthrough video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=306cqZcS9D4) shows every step above in 11 minutes.
